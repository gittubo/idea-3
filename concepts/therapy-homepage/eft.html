What is EFT?

Built on what we know about attachment

EFT was developed in the 1980s by Dr Sue Johnson and Dr Les Greenberg. It draws on attachment theory: the understanding that, throughout our lives, we are wired to seek closeness and comfort from the people who matter most.

When that connection feels uncertain, we protest. Some of us reach harder, criticise or pursue. Others protect themselves by going quiet or pulling away. Neither partner is the problem. The pattern is.

EFT doesn't teach couples to argue better. It helps them feel safe enough that they don't need to.

EFT is one of the most thoroughly researched forms of couples therapy, and it's now also used with individuals (EFIT) and families (EFFT).

How it works

Three stages of change

  1. Stage one

    De-escalation

    We identify the negative cycle that takes over when you feel disconnected, and see how each of you gets caught in it. As you start to see the cycle as the enemy, conflict begins to soften.

  2. Stage two

    Restructuring the bond

    We slowly explore the feelings and needs beneath the reactions: fear of not mattering, of not being enough, of being left. Sharing these, and being met, creates new moments of closeness.

  3. Stage three

    Consolidation

    With a safer bond, old problems become easier to solve together. We strengthen the new patterns so they last long after therapy ends.

About EFT

If you'd like to read more, Resources has a reading list, including Hold Me Tight by Sue Johnson.

Is EFT evidence-based?

Yes. EFT has been studied for more than three decades, and research consistently supports its effectiveness for couples in distress.

Will we just talk about feelings?

Feelings are central, but the work is structured and practical. We focus on what happens between you in real moments, and practise new ways of responding.

Does EFT work if one of us is more committed?

Often, yes. It's common for partners to arrive with different levels of hope. EFT helps both of you feel heard, which tends to bring hesitant partners in.

Can EFT help with trauma?

EFT is often used alongside trauma-informed care. Feeling secure with someone you love can be a powerful part of healing.
